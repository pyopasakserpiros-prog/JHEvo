## What the owner wants to play

Give NPCs a world with a past that they can encounter, interpret, argue over, remember, pass on, and sometimes act upon. Put lore in the running game through encounters with people, oral stories, school lineages, inscriptions or objects, archives, reports, findings in the field, and other channels you design. The observer should be able to follow credible chains from a scene or conversation to a later decision and its consequences. Quiet scenes, everyday local events, flawed recollections and culture that serves no material purpose are valuable. Some discoveries should also let an interested NPC take meaningful action through a live system. Use the amount of content and depth needed to make an entire release feel like a more inhabited world.

**Gemini owns the final design and implementation.** Read the lore closely, choose what deserves to become canon or playable, and independently invent stronger stories, events and ways to connect systems. Look for omissions and weak transitions across time, place, factions, NPC motives and established mechanics. Write original lore to fill useful gaps, sharpen distinctive ideas from the proposals, and preserve mysteries that gain from remaining open. Improve, combine, replace or decline Codex's ideas with reasons where the choice materially affects the game. Explore existing game features for lore possibilities absent from both the sources and this prompt. Implement your original ideas in the playable release as well. Treat the design handoff as a collaborator's contribution with full design discretion under AGENTS.md.

## What to build

1. **Read and reconcile the lore.** Read the master lore, the full lore families indexed in the source map, and relevant event, court, settlement and content proposals. Search the branch for additional lore and verify against actual source and the generated build. Track what is established, what you adopt or revise, what each source claims in-world, and what remains unknown. Compare era, geography, motives, historical status, ownership and existing acquisition. Published facts, local oral traditions and propaganda can all have distinct accounts. Resolve intentional disagreements in the design; keep accidental contradictions out of canonical history.
2. **Make learning and transmission happen in the world.** Use reasoned exposure from visit, conversation, mentorship, witnessing, reading, examination, or another credible source. Track knowledge by NPC when it matters. Reuse bounded clue, cognition and chronicle primitives where they fit. Let a belief change with evidence, disputed accounts, comprehension, trust and time. A storyteller knows what they could have learned. Repeating one story through multiple intermediaries is one evidence lineage. Archive, knowledge and report UI should let the observer see who knows what, why they know it and what was observed or reported, with secrets gated by discovery.
3. **Build varied follow-through.** Give NPCs reasons to ask for another account, argue, teach, change routes, visit settlements, investigate, seek training or research, trade, protect or exploit a finding, or ignore it. Build several playable stories with different consequences and independent paths. Reuse meaningful locations and add selected sites or interiors where an idea calls for one. Link relevant actual techniques, gear, materials, pills, recipes, Dao paths and subpaths, beasts, companions, cities, polities, court positions and other systems you find useful. Include knowledge-only encounters, advantages such as safer decisions or relationships, and rare tangible finds with plausible sources. Audit both directions of every important link: how an NPC reaches it and what actually happens afterward. A one-off clue can remain special if the sim can still resolve cleanly when its owner dies, refuses to speak or leaves.
4. **Make events arise from the simulation.** Implement both ordinary life and lore-driven events. Decide which triggers belong to local activity, status or thresholds, which require a witness or knowledge, and which should have rare seeded opportunities. Give events appropriate actors, locations, timing, cooldowns, reactions and follow-up. Reuse market, work, travel, family, relationships, governance and cultivation outcomes where useful. Register the causal state, then produce Thai chronicle entries and map links from real witnesses or event snapshots. Respect news settings and visibility. Avoid forced story outcomes, mass knowledge, and simultaneous duplicate resolutions.
5. **Preserve the living-world contract.** NPCs decide based on personality, abilities, aims, apprehension, interests, beliefs, training, commitments and available resources. Two NPCs can learn the same story and respond differently. Waiting, forgetting, cutting losses or declining can be valid. Keep the player in the observer role with the existing Edit Hub permissions. Use existing travel and map coordinates at 1,000 × 1,000 li and 10 li/unit. Optional sites should be real destinations or discoverable interactions within that geography. Route feasibility, time, factions, seasons and risk should count. Group transportation is [deferred](docs/deferred/GROUP_TRANSPORTATION_PROPOSAL.md).
6. **Follow consequences across systems.** Trace who could know and when, who can act, how often, what it costs, and who benefits. Consider scarce evidence, downtime, competent staffing, injury, heirlooms, contested ownership, loot scarcity, duplicate routes, payoffs, trading and training, long-term accumulation, and different seeds. Preserve authentic unique artifacts and eggs through their existing source and owner rules. Let real materials satisfy real uses; apply ordinary progression and prerequisites when lore helps someone discover a technique or route. Ensure habit and risk choices stay human, repeated low-value events stay quiet, and rare events are reachable under valid conditions.

Work in stages if helpful. A strong release has breadth across the old and new systems and depth in the individual stories. Use your judgment on what deserves a full chain, a short encounter, a quiet entry, or a future hook. Prioritize finished runtime integration over theoretical catalog size.
