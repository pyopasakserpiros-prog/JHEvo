# JS2 v3.1.0 Living Lore — Codex design proposals for Gemini

These are new story and systems proposals against the v3.0.0 source. Place IDs are real anchors. Proposed people, names, incidents, local customs, uses and rewards require your design and source verification. Start with the [active assignment](../../ACTIVE_PATCH_PROMPT_V3_1_0.md) and [lore source map](JS2_V3_1_0_LORE_SOURCE_MAP.md). Gemini has the final say and should invent and ship original ideas of its own.

## Where the current lore could grow

| Gap | Possible addition | What the sim gains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Most histories are accounts of rulers, the strong and wars | Give surviving toll keepers, port families, medics, apprentices and retired caravan hands their own names for an old event. Record where the accounts agree. | NPCs make believable mistakes about their own history. A credible account can survive through a family, a trade or an outlying town. |
| Founding and succession drafts can make the current sect leader feel like a stand-in for the founder | Give each generation a practical problem and a record of what they changed. For example, one generation opened the outer courtyard during a famine and another restricted archive access after a forgery. Keep first founder and Generation 7 distinct. | The present leader has actual responsibilities and inherited relationships. Different NPCs can learn different parts of the history without being handed a flat timeline. |
| Dao philosophies can read like labels on progression trees | Write moments where practitioners of the same major path disagree about a method for understandable reasons. Show the distinction in an apprenticeship, recovery or field technique. | Subpaths have a cultural and mechanical identity. Ability and conviction explain why one NPC seeks a teacher another would refuse. |
| Ancient catastrophe and the Upper World crowd out recent memory | Give a recent border change two anniversaries and one consistent mundane physical trace, such as a reused milestone or a lost family route. Keep pre-Tiangu and beyond-fog claims in mutually incomplete records. | Some mysteries can be settled with testimony and a journey. Rare cosmological clues can keep their scale without every farming town knowing the truth. |
| Beast kings and relics dominate the bestiary and item histories | Write stories about ordinary species and their materials, failed expeditions, and maintenance of inherited tools. Make some historical names regional names for existing objects if the data supports it. | Low-stage characters can encounter and act on lore. Crafting origins and reasons for seeking an item become legible at multiple tiers. |
| Gazetteers blur political control, culture and personal loyalty | Give market days, seasonal crossings and schools a different reach from court decrees. NPCs from neighboring states can share a trade custom or disagree with their own government. | Borders and hometowns affect individual choices without becoming fixed group behavior. |

The best gap to fill is often a transition between established facts. Ask who preserved the old fact, where it survived, why somebody cares now and what they could actually do. Keep the names of ancient unknown powers, the true reach of the Veil, and private motives unresolved wherever a direct answer would make the setting poorer.

## Original playable story seeds

### 1. The flood ledger at Shuangjiang

A retired boatwright tells apprentices that there was a year in which the river flowed backwards. A dry-season stone mark near Shuangjiang (`CITY_YH_SHUANGJIANG`) records an ordinary flood, and a surviving ledger marks emergency grain movements on the same dates. Both sources can be sincere. A merchant may check a reputable archive or the Sanjiang crossing (`PASS_YH_SANJIANG_BRIDGE`) before risking a convoy. A local child may remember the river story simply because it explains the festival lanterns. A route delay or protective change must come from the live routing and logistics state. A later chronicler can credit the boatwright for remembering the danger while correcting the literal reading of the story.

### 2. Two hands annotated the same manual

At Bailu Academy (`CITY_YH_BAILU_ACADEMY`), one copy of a teaching record has two different approaches to the same difficulty. It preserves an actual schism from the Dao sources, with each author shown solving a different problem. Choose a live path, two compatible training options and applicable manuals or techniques from source. An NPC with a blocked goal might ask a mentor, examine the alternate teaching or stay loyal to a lineage. A learner still needs access to an actual technique, teacher, practice and the regular prerequisites. A curious apprentice who cannot learn either approach can transmit the historical disagreement or discover a suitable mentor. A master copying one annotation for a disciple risks friction with another teacher, not a global relationship penalty.

### 3. The second toll stamp

Donghuang's Qinglan market (`CITY_DH_QINGLAN_DICE_CITY`) still uses an old trading stamp, while a surviving official record or ruined prefectural archive (`CITY_DH_ZHENYANG_RUIN_PREF`) attributes an earlier stamp to a fallen regime. A shipment with conflicting marks can trigger a private inquiry if there is a knowledgeable broker, copied record or applicable customs office. A merchant might delay delivery, accept a credible witness, or take a loss rather than risk the next contract. A proof changes the treatment of that shipment or a local trust relationship once. Reuse actual inventory, delivery state and diplomacy. Avoid repeating a profit from a cached report or treating a ruin as enough to validate every stamped shipment.

### 4. A boundary named by two villages

At Yuguan (`PASS_YH_YUGUAN_RUIN_POST`), two local stories honor different families for guiding refugees through a gate. A contemporary official may interpret an inscription as a claim to jurisdiction; an older courier may see it as a trail sign. Townspeople who actually interact can teach either account. A traveler may consult a second witness, decide to help repair a marker, or choose a better understood route. Use living history and the existing city/court model. An old inscription alone should never hand a current office to a family, move the border or force all residents into a common opinion. Investigating can remain worthwhile for trust, local access or a more accurate account.

### 5. The beast keeper's notebook

A faded field note records when a local aquatic or riverbank species leaves signs along a ford. Select a species that actually spawns or is reachable through a current encounter system and check its attributes and material uses. A risk-averse scavenger may use the note to avoid a dangerous time and a hunter may schedule a properly provisioned trip. An alchemist could request a regular drop and a keeper may object to a hunt near a nesting site. Actual combat, drops and collection use the live systems. The knowledge could save a novice an injury even if they never hunt. Use the same principle for a land beast, a flying companion lineage or an ecology tale with no unlock. A companion egg or sovereign beast still uses its normal origin and bonding rules.

### 6. A shrine inside an old storeroom

A worker replacing a damaged shelf finds an unremarkable maker's mark below a floorboard. A seamstress or quartermaster can tell why that mark appears on common protective gear. Tie it to one real piece of equipment, one ordinary material and a faction or craft tradition with a trade motive. Possible outcomes include a repair method supported by a real crafting recipe, an apprentice conversation, a supplier named in a record, or merely a corrected name in an inventory note. The contents of a room, worker time and repair cost are actual game state where the existing systems support them. The site can be a discovered micro-location on an existing node.

### 7. The empty chair in the seventh generation

One earlier sect leader left a practice of leaving a seat open during a memorial. A senior treats it as archive etiquette, and a retiring steward understands it as a promise about succession. Find compatible fragments in the Gen7 and neutral heirloom drafts and reconcile them with shipped succession logic. The present Gen7 leader, the first founder and any sealed relic carry separate identities. A trusted mentor can disclose a fragment, a claimant might hold it privately, and an observer can learn more through real changes in the sect. The story can explain why an existing inheritance rule exists. An inscription supplies evidence or a motive; the genuine heirloom still changes hands only under current ownership and succession rules.

### 8. A sketched trail with a missing bend

A worn sketch shows two landmarks that a historical author interpreted as traces of the Tiangu war. Set one confirmed clue on a current route. Another might be in an existing sanctum, subject to its existing conditions. A confident explorer could confuse two similar ridges, an experienced guide might identify a safer approach, and a companion can withdraw when injured. Most investigations may reveal useful route knowledge, a fragment of a named figure's biography or the reason a shrine was abandoned. If you tie a chain to a sovereign find, use one existing acquisition authority and the proper tier requirements. A rival arriving first, a dead witness or an artifact that already has an owner should change the follow-up. A rare discovery can remain reachable without predestined success or exposing Upper World facts to everyone.

### 9. One salt line, two ports

At Haimen (`CITY_DH_HAIMEN_PORT`) or Pearl Isle (`CITY_SO_NANZHU_PEARL_ISLE`), two accounts of a past shortage blame each other's merchants. Nearby records can show that weather and a delayed ferry explained part of it. Use routine ferry links and existing local market signals. A cautious NPC may stock a currently needed common input before a risk period, while an impulsive trader dismisses the story. A friend may pass on the practical lesson after a missed connection. This is a useful market event even if nobody solves the old dispute. It also provides a place for a maritime story before the shared-transport expansion.

### 10. The name restored to a damaged memorial

An incomplete stone or family copy names a medic, artisan or courier who lived during a proposed lost dynasty. One person wrongly connects the name to a famous surviving figure. An NPC initially sees a record about someone presumed dead, missing or forgotten; a second record distinguishes them with an era, place or trade. When people with reliable evidence give an account, the chronicle can resolve the attribution and a family relationship or attitude may change. Select already reconciled figures or author a modest historical person with a reason to have been omitted. A historical record should never spawn a live duplicate, declare a present NPC guilty or assign a title without the normal proof.

### 11. The meal paid twice

An everyday event starts after an NPC pays for a communal meal and a second person covers it again out of habit or uncertainty. A proud cook might return the extra payment; a suspicious worker asks who saw the payment; a forgetful or generous NPC lets it pass. Use a real contribution or transaction if available, or keep the event as witnessed conversation and a modest relationship change. In some towns the old custom of quietly setting aside food for injured travelers gives the moment a different meaning. The scene can run with no lore discovery at all. Only real witnesses can later tell it as a witnessed event.

### 12. The report over the wrong ridge

A court scout or trader reports a beast movement or blocked route. The witness was on a known road, but poor light left two plausible locations. A court officer or adequate local responder may send word on the route they trust, prioritize their other duty, or seek a second witness. Another NPC can decline to join if short on medicine or training. Use the live threat or travel state and the stable atlas route snapshot. Confirmation should correct the map position or leave it as an unverified regional report. An incident can produce a short chronicle about cooperation without requiring any special loot or a large battle.

## Choose how knowledge can be used

A useful content review asks what each fact unlocks **for someone who actually learns it**. A possible set of outcomes follows.

| Use | Examples | Boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Culture or memory | Recite a saying, preserve a hometown name, observe a rite, tell an apprentice about a dead founder | Optional with quiet frequency and an appropriate audience |
| Evidence | Ask someone, compare registers, join an expedition to look for traces, correct a rumor | Independent origins count separately; conflicting accounts can remain unresolved |
| Judgement | Better understand risk, prefer a guide, change a route, shelter, or delay until prepared | Existing ability, costs and limits still matter |
| Relationship | Place trust in someone, share with a family member or mentor, repair civic reputation | Modest and local unless wider evidence and credible transmission exist |
| Progression | Look for a mentor or technique, research a recipe, study a compatible subpath | Use real learning, access, inputs and progression |
| Physical discovery | Examine a separate site, recover a mundane item, earn the normal drop or rare authentic find | Normal reward authority, no duplication, correct ownership, no free inventory from a rumor |

## Implementation decisions to make before adding lots of content

- **What persists:** Consider a stable fact or account ID with source and discovery requirements, and an NPC's compact evidence history with origin, credibility, first encounter and a remembered status. Important story steps may reference live NPCs, actual nodes, transactions and chronicle snapshots. Choose a representation that survives save/reload without materializing every pair of NPC and fact. Apply bounds and indexed due work.
- **Who says what:** Keep author truth, audience visibility, NPC belief and observed event records distinct. A speaker can be sincere and wrong. Independent witnesses can improve confidence; one copied claim stays linked to its original source. Records can be public, local or shared privately on relevant schedules. Let knowledge grant options and use the existing autonomy model to weigh likely benefit, travel, fear, competing obligations and attachments. Give listeners meaningful reasons to doubt, decline, wait or stop.
- **What changes the world:** A lore trigger should enter at the system responsible for the action or outcome. Resolve actual costs and ownership there, once. Prefer transitions such as heard → checked → attempted → outcome where useful, with visible evidence and quiet exit paths. Avoid a single repeating global roll, duplicate gains from two story paths or a route that resolves before physical arrival. A knowledge-only scene can be one transition.
- **What the observer sees:** Consider a short Thai “heard from,” “saw at,” or “followed up” link within existing actor, world and chronicle surfaces. In a meaningful chain show what motivated the trip, what was found and whether somebody else later learned it. Handle moved or deceased people using snapshots, support compact mobile inspection, and reveal sites at the right resolution. Never expose hidden coordinates or the complete author history by opening an observer view.
- **What could break later:** Trace accumulation when expertise spreads over generations, repeated stories fill memory, new polities join, or a once-rare road becomes busy. Test thin worlds and strong worlds, fast simulation, weak and expert NPCs, early and late starts, night/season changes, unaffordable trips, contradictory reports and multiple eligible claimants. Chronicle data that used to be private should keep its visibility if it is saved and later presented.

For your own ideas, look at actual systems these examples barely touch, such as breakthroughs, kinship, property and labor, court ambitions, epidemic or recovery histories, vessel folklore, relic maintenance, and conflicting epithet stories. Let the existing code and lore shape fresh ideas. Keep a brief record of which Codex proposals you kept, changed or left aside and which original Gemini contributions reached live play.
